# Unitary Evolution & Operator Exponentials

## Stone's Theorem: The Crown Jewel

**Theorem (Stone, 1932).** There is a bijective correspondence between:

* **Strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups** $\{U(t)\}_{t \in \mathbb{R}}$ on $\mathcal{H}$ (i.e., $U(t+s) = U(t)U(s)$, $U(0) = I$, $t \mapsto U(t)\psi$ continuous for all $\psi$)

* **Self-adjoint operators** $\hat{H}$ on $\mathcal{H}$

via $U(t) = e^{-it\hat{H}}$ (defined by the spectral theorem), with $\hat{H}$ recovered as the **infinitesimal generator**:

$$
\hat{H}\psi = i\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{U(t)\psi - \psi}{t}, \qquad D(\hat{H}) = \left\\{\psi : \text{this limit exists}\right\\}
$$

---

## Proof Sketch of Stone's Theorem

:::matrix { cols="50/50" rows="100" height="80%"}

[[0, 0]]
**Direction 1** ($\hat{H} \Rightarrow U(t)$):

Given self-adjoint $\hat{H}$, define $U(t) = e^{-it\hat{H}}$ via spectral calculus:

$$U(t) = \int_{\sigma(\hat{H})} e^{-it\lambda}\\, dE_\lambda$$

**Unitarity**: $U(t)^* = \int e^{it\lambda}\\, dE_\lambda = U(-t) = U(t)^{-1}$ ✓

**Group property**: $U(t)U(s) = \int e^{-i(t+s)\lambda}\\, dE_\lambda = U(t+s)$ ✓

**Strong continuity**: $\|U(t)\psi - \psi\|^2 = \int |e^{-it\lambda} - 1|^2\, d\|E_\lambda\psi\|^2 \to 0$ by dominated convergence ✓

[[0, 1]]
**Direction 2** ($U(t) \Rightarrow \hat{H}$):

Given a strongly continuous unitary group $U(t)$:

1. Define $\hat{H}\psi = i\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{U(t)\psi - \psi}{t}$ on the domain where this limit exists

2. Show $D(\hat{H})$ is dense (use mollified vectors $\psi_f = \int f(t)U(t)\psi\, dt$ with $f \in C_c^\infty$)

3. Show $\hat{H}$ is symmetric: $\langle \hat{H}\phi|\psi\rangle = \langle\phi|\hat{H}\psi\rangle$ (from unitarity of $U(t)$)

4. Show $\hat{H}$ is self-adjoint by proving $\text{Ran}(\hat{H} \pm iI) = \mathcal{H}$ (the **deficiency indices** are both zero)

5. Uniqueness: spectral theorem reconstructs $U(t)$ ✓

:::

## Physical Content of Stone's Theorem

The **Schrödinger equation** $i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle = \hat{H}|\psi(t)\rangle$ is **exactly equivalent** to the statement that time evolution is a one-parameter unitary group generated by $\hat{H}$.

**Three immediate consequences:**

1. **Probability conservation**: $\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi(t)\|^2 = 0$ because $U(t)$ is an isometry

2. **Deterministic between measurements**: given $|\psi(0)\rangle$, the future state is uniquely determined by $|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|\psi(0)\rangle$

3. **Time-reversal**: $U(-t) = U(t)^{-1} = U(t)^\dagger$ — evolution is reversible (before measurement)

> Self-adjointness of $\hat{H}$ is not merely a convenient property — it is the **necessary and sufficient condition** for the existence of a well-defined time evolution. A merely symmetric $\hat{H}$ does not generate a unitary group.

## Operator Exponentials and Lie Theory

For a **bounded** operator $\hat{A}$ (or a matrix), the exponential is well-defined by Taylor series:

$$
e^{\hat{A}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{\hat{A}^n}{n!} = \hat{I} + \hat{A} + \frac{1}{2}\hat{A}^2 + \frac{1}{6}\hat{A}^3 + \cdots
$$

This converges in operator norm. The map $\hat{A} \mapsto e^{\hat{A}}$ is the **exponential map** from the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ to the Lie group $G$.

**Key question:** When does $e^{\hat{A}}e^{\hat{B}} = e^{\hat{A}+\hat{B}}$?

**Answer:** If and only if $[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = 0$.

In quantum mechanics, $\hat{H} = \hat{T} + \hat{V}$, but $[\hat{T}, \hat{V}] \neq 0$. Therefore:

$$
e^{-i(\hat{T}+\hat{V})\Delta t} \\;\neq\\; e^{-i\hat{T}\Delta t}\cdot e^{-i\hat{V}\Delta t}
$$

This non-commutativity is not a nuisance — it is the **entire content** of quantum dynamics.

## The Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff Formula

**Theorem (BCH).** For operators $\hat{A}, \hat{B}$ in a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$:

$$
e^{\hat{A}}e^{\hat{B}} = e^{\hat{Z}}, \qquad \hat{Z} = \hat{A} + \hat{B} + \frac{1}{2}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] + \frac{1}{12}\bigl([\hat{A},[\hat{A},\hat{B}]] - [\hat{B},[\hat{A},\hat{B}]]\bigr) + \cdots
$$

The infinite series $\hat{Z}$ involves **only commutators** (nested Lie brackets) — no products $\hat{A}\hat{B}$ outside brackets.

> **Theorem (Dynkin).** $\hat{Z}$ is a Lie series: it lies in the **free Lie algebra** generated by $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{B}$. This means the product of Lie group elements can be expressed entirely in terms of the Lie algebra structure.

**Special case:** If $[\hat{A}, [\hat{A}, \hat{B}]] = [\hat{B}, [\hat{A}, \hat{B}]] = 0$ (i.e., the commutator $[\hat{A}, \hat{B}]$ is central):

$$
e^{\hat{A}}e^{\hat{B}} = e^{\hat{A}+\hat{B}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{A},\hat{B}]}
$$

This is the **Weyl identity**, used in the Stone–von Neumann proof.

## Trotter Product Formula & the Split-Operator Method

**Theorem (Trotter, 1959).** For self-adjoint operators $\hat{A}, \hat{B}$ such that $\hat{A} + \hat{B}$ is essentially self-adjoint on $D(\hat{A}) \cap D(\hat{B})$:

$$
e^{t(\hat{A}+\hat{B})} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(e^{t\hat{A}/n}\\, e^{t\hat{B}/n}\right)^n
$$

**Finite-order approximations (from Zassenhaus):**

:::matrix { cols="50/50" rows="100" height="50%"}

[[0, 0]]
**First-order (Lie-Trotter):**
$$e^{\Delta t(\hat{A}+\hat{B})} = e^{\Delta t\hat{A}}\\,e^{\Delta t\hat{B}} + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$$

Error: $\sim \frac{\Delta t^2}{2}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}]$

[[0, 1]]
**Second-order (Strang/symmetric):**
$$e^{\Delta t(\hat{A}+\hat{B})} = e^{\frac{\Delta t}{2}\hat{A}}\\,e^{\Delta t\hat{B}}\\,e^{\frac{\Delta t}{2}\hat{A}} + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^3)$$

The symmetric splitting **cancels** the $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$ error term.

:::

## From Trotter-Suzuki to the Split-Operator Method

Setting $\hat{A} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{V}$ and $\hat{B} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{T}$ in the second-order Trotter formula:

$$
\boxed{e^{-i\hat{H}\Delta t/\hbar} \approx e^{-i\hat{V}\Delta t/2\hbar} \cdot e^{-i\hat{T}\Delta t/\hbar} \cdot e^{-i\hat{V}\Delta t/2\hbar} + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^3)}
$$

**Why this is computationally powerful:**

* $\hat{V}$ is a **multiplication operator** in position space — $e^{-i\hat{V}\Delta t/2\hbar}$ acts by pointwise multiplication
* $\hat{T} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}$ is a **multiplication operator** in momentum space — $e^{-i\hat{T}\Delta t/\hbar}$ acts by pointwise multiplication in $k$-space
* Switching between representations: the **Fourier transform** (FFT), cost $\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$

Each time step: half-kick in $x$-space → FFT → drift in $k$-space → IFFT → half-kick in $x$-space.
