\section*{Problem: Learning the Time-Evolution Operator}

The split-operator method is exact but sequential: predicting $\psi(x, t)$ requires stepping through every intermediate time. A \textbf{Fourier Neural Operator} (FNO) learns to approximate the time-evolution operator directly, giving $\mathcal{O}(1)$ inference regardless of target time.

\subsection*{Mathematical Background}

\textbf{Spectral Convolution.} A general integral operator $(\mathcal{K}v)(x) = \int \kappa(x,y)\,v(y)\,dy$ requires $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ parameters. If the kernel is translation-invariant, the Convolution Theorem gives:
$$
\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{K}(v)](k) = \hat{\kappa}(k) \cdot \hat{v}(k)
$$
The FNO learns the Fourier-space kernel $R(k) \in \mathbb{C}^{c_\text{in} \times c_\text{out} \times k_\text{max}}$ directly, with cost $\mathcal{O}(k_\text{max})$ instead of $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$.

\textbf{FNO Layer:}
$$
v_{\ell+1}(x) = \sigma\!\left( W\,v_\ell(x) + \mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left[R \cdot \mathcal{F}(v_\ell)\right](x) \right)
$$
where $W$ is a local bypass (1$\times$1 convolution), $R$ is the learnable spectral weight, and $\sigma$ is GeLU.
