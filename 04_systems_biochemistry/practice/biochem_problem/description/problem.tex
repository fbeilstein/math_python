\section*{Problem 4: Saccharomyces cerevisiae Glycolytic Oscillations}

\subsubsection*{Mathematical part}
The yeast glycolysis feedback loop (Bier-Bakker-Westerhoff model) consists of three phenomenological processes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Glucose influx:} External glucose enters the cell at a constant zero-order rate $V_{in}$.
    $$ \emptyset\ce{->[V_{in}] G} $$
    
    \item \textbf{Autocatalytic Glycolysis (PFK step):} Glucose is phosphorylated by ATP with rate $k_1 \cdot G \cdot ATP$. In this simplified model, the entire payoff phase of glycolysis is lumped into this step, yielding a \emph{net production} of 2 ATP per glucose consumed.
    $$ \ce{G + ATP ->[k_1] 3 ATP} $$
    \emph{(Note: Consuming 1 ATP and producing 3 yields a net of +2 ATP, take this into account when constructing the differential equation).}
    
    \item \textbf{ATP consumption:} ATP is consumed by downstream metabolism with saturating Michaelis-Menten kinetics.
    \begin{center}
    	\includesvg[width=0.5\textwidth]{images/ATP_ADP}
    \end{center}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Task:} Write the ODEs for $G$ and $ATP$. Use parameters $V_{in} = 0.36$, $k_1 = 0.02$, $k_p = 6.0$. Analytically solve for the fixed point $(G_{eq}, ATP_{eq})$ by setting $dG/dt = 0$ and $dATP/dt = 0$.

\textbf{Hint:} The feedback arrow from ATP to the PFK reaction means that ATP is both a \emph{substrate} (consumed) and a \emph{product} (produced) of that reaction step. This autocatalytic feedback is what generates the oscillations.

\textbf{The main task:} The Goldbeter-Koshland zero-order ultrasensitivity mutual activation switch is
\begin{center}
  \includesvg[width=0.8\textwidth]{images/problem_5}
\end{center}
Write the ODEs for $R$ and $E$. Use parameters: $k_{R0} = 0.22$, $k_{R1} = 0.001$, $k_{E0} = 0.8$, $k_{E1} = 0.01$, $K_0 = 0.01$, $K_1 = 0.01$, $E_{tot} = 0.5$.


\textbf{Why this is interesting:} The network topology features a Response $R$ and an Enzyme $E$ that mutually activate each other, forming a true bistable positive feedback loop. In the presence of a transient stimulus $V(t)$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R$ is produced at a basal rate catalyzed by the active enzyme $E_p$. Since the precursor supply is infinite ($\emptyset$), the rate is linear with respect to $E_p$: $k_{R0} \cdot E_p$. This is supplemented by the external stimulus $V(t)$.
    \item $R$ degrades linearly at rate $k_{R1} [R]$.
    \item $R$ acts as a kinase, phosphorylating the inactive enzyme $E \to E_p$ with Michaelis-Menten kinetics.
    \item A constitutive phosphatase $X$ (with constant concentration $[X]=1$) dephosphorylates $E_p \to E$ with Michaelis-Menten kinetics.
\end{itemize}

Because the Michaelis constants $K_0, K_1$ are extremely small compared to $E_{tot}$, the enzyme operates in the zero-order saturated regime. This generates the steep ultrasensitive response necessary for biochemical memory (hysteresis).

\section*{Problem 6: Cell Cycle Checkpoint (Cusp Catastrophe)}

\subsubsection*{Mathematical part}

The cell cycle checkpoint is governed by the interplay between Cyclin and Wee1 kinase. We can build a phenomenological model directly from the following effective rates. 

\textbf{Note loud and clear:} This is a topological normal form (the canonical cusp catastrophe), not a set of elementary mass-action reactions! The variables $Cyclin$ and $Wee1$ act as mathematical bifurcation control parameters (the normal and splitting factors, respectively), not physical enzymatic rate constants.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Production (Normal factor):} Cyclin drives the basal production of active Cdc2 ($C$). This is mathematically modeled as a zero-order influx where the rate $V_1$ is directly parameterized by the Cyclin concentration.
    $$ \emptyset\ce{->[V] C} $$

    \item \textbf{Autocatalysis (Splitting factor):} Wee1 interacts with $C$ to create a positive feedback loop. This is modeled as an autocatalytic influx where the rate $V_2$ is proportional to $C$, with Wee1 acting as the apparent rate coefficient.
    $$ \ce{C ->[k] 2C}$$

    \item \textbf{Degradation:} $C$ undergoes a phenomenological high-order, nonlinear self-inhibition.
    $$ \ce{3C ->[1] \emptyset}$$
\end{itemize}

Derive the differential equation for $[C]$.
The equilibrium manifold is a folded surface in $(k, V, C)$ space.
When a trajectory crosses the fold boundary, the system undergoes a \textbf{catastrophic jump} to the other stable branch.


\textbf{Task:} Find the steady-state concentration(s) of $C_\text{stable}$.
You should get a cubic polynomial, there may be up to 3 real roots (bistability). 
To account for \textbf{hysteresis} (biochemical memory), your function must evaluate all roots and return the real root that is \emph{closest} to the system's current state $C_\text{current}$.
